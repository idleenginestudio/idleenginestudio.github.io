One-line pitch

A vault-cracking math game with no digits on screen: numbers are shown only as dot patterns, and every combination is the "shadow" of the puzzle, a near-mirror that is never quite symmetric.

Concept

Multiplying by 9, 99 or 999 has a famous shortcut. Take one away from the number and write that as the front half. For the back half, write each digit's gap to 9:

47 × 99 → front 46, back 53 → 4653

Most games would show this as a formula. Shadow Vault instead turns the trick into the picture. Each digit is a 3×3 block of dots, and the back half of the answer is literally the empty space (the shadow) of the front half. Put a front block and its back block together and the grid is exactly full. The player learns the trick by seeing it, not by reading it.

2. Theme: Imperfect Symmetry

The whole game is built on one shape that is almost symmetric.

  • Mirror, with a missing piece. The vault's front half and back half are opposites. Every lit dot in front matches an empty dot behind, so the pairs fill the grid perfectly. But the front half is not the puzzle itself; it is the puzzle minus one dot. That one missing dot is what breaks the symmetry, and it is the key to every lock.
  • The shadow is the answer. Light and shadow, filled and empty, front and back: the answer is always the puzzle's negative image, shifted by one.
  • Breaking the mirror on purpose. In the Master Vault, the puzzle is longer than the multiplier, so the number must be cut and the halves no longer line up. The player has to work out where the symmetry breaks and repair it by hand: one less here, one more there.

The theme is not decoration added on top. It is the rule the player has to discover to win.

The math behind it

Multiplying by a row of nines is a subtraction in disguise. For a two-digit N:

N × 99 = N × 100 − N = (N − 1) × 100 + (100 − N)

  • The left part, N − 1, is the front half: the puzzle minus one dot.
  • The right part, 100 − N = 99 − (N − 1), takes each digit of the front away from 9. That is the front's empty space, its shadow.

The same holds for ×9 and ×999 with one or three slots. In the Master Vault, N has more digits than the nines, so the shifted number and the subtraction overlap. Cut N into a left part and a right part (as many digits as the nines): the front becomes N − left − 1 and the back becomes 10^k − right. Example: 1234 × 99 → front 1234 − 12 − 1 = 1221, back 100 − 34 = 66 → 122166. Every puzzle the game generates was checked against N × (10^k − 1).

3. Core Gameplay

Core loop

  1. Read the puzzle: dot blocks × full blocks (1 full block = ×9, 2 = ×99, 3 = ×999).
  2. Think in your head, because no digits are ever shown.
  3. Dial the answer: select a block on the vault door and place dots on the dot keypad.
  4. Unlock before the fuse burns out, then go to the next lock.

Rules

  • Numbers = dot blocks. A 3×3 block with n dots lit means the digit n; an empty block means zero.
  • Front (gold) = puzzle minus one.
  • Back (cyan) = the shadow of the front: each block's empty dots.
  • Big numbers (Master Vault): cut the puzzle so the right part has as many blocks as the full blocks.
  • Front = puzzle − left part − 1
  • Back = shadow of the right part + 1
  • Mistakes: a wrong answer or a timeout costs a heart. The game then shows the correct vault, marks the wrong blocks, and repeats the unlocking rule.

  • Every level opens with a short animated tutorial that shows the trick with dots. It can be replayed from the map.
  • Bulb hints reveal the answer for the currently selected block (2 per level, 3 in Endless).

5. The No-Digit Rule (UI Design)

The game's biggest design constraint is that no numerals appear anywhere: no Arabic digits and no Chinese number characters. Every counter is a picture instead.

Information Shown as
Time left A burning fuse
Lives Hearts
Question progress A row of small padlocks
Combo Flames
Endless score Gems (1 big gem = 10 small gems)
Star rating Stars

Even the interface text avoids number words, so the player is always the one turning pictures into numbers.

6. Art Direction

  • Soft clay toy look: a mint background, chunky pastel cards and buttons with a pressed-clay shine, and a yellow toy safe with little feet.
  • Each digit block is a chocolate tray of candy dots. The front half uses orange candies on a pink panel and the back half uses mint candies on a mint panel, so "light vs. shadow" reads as two flavors of the same tray.
  • Lock tiers keep their material colors (bronze, silver, gold, jade, ruby) on flower-shaped clay badges.
  • The timer is a rope fuse with a spark; hints come from a glowing bulb.
  • Font: Huninn, a rounded Traditional Chinese typeface that also covers Latin.
  • Every visual is drawn in code (CSS/SVG), so it stays sharp on any screen and the build stays small.
  • The style was chosen on 2026-11-02 from five concept directions: brass vault, neon vault, Chinese puzzle box, soft clay toy and paper-cut shadows.
  • Mobile-first layout, tested at 360×740 and 390×844.

7. Audio

All sound effects are synthesized live with Web Audio: dot taps, unlock chimes, a jammed-lock buzz, fuse warning ticks, and combo flourishes. No audio files are used. A mute toggle is always available.

8. Technical Details

  • A single self-contained HTML file (about 130 KB) with an embedded, subsetted font. There are no external requests.
  • Progress and settings are saved in the browser (localStorage).
  • Puzzle generation was verified by test: 20,000 random questions across all five levels, each answer equal to N × (10^k − 1).
